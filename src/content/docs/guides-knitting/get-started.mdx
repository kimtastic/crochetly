Knitting uses two needles to create fabric from yarn. If you already crochet, you already understand yarn, tension, and gauge — the main difference is that knitting holds multiple live stitches on the needle at once. This guide covers the essentials to start your first project.

## What tools do I need to start knitting?

You only need a few basic supplies to begin:

- **Yarn:** Beginners start well with medium worsted weight (#4) in a light, solid color
- **Knitting Needles:** US size 8 (5 mm) straight or circular needles
- **Tapestry Needle:** For weaving in ends
- **Stitch Markers:** To mark places and keep track of sections
- **Stitch Holders:** To hold stitches you are not currently working
- **Row Counter:** To track how many rows you have knit
- **Scissors**

## What basic knitting terms should I know?

| Term | Meaning | Crochet comparison |
|------|---------|--------------------|
| Cast On `CO` | Creating the foundation row of stitches onto the needle | Like a foundation chain |
| Knit `K` | The most basic knitting stitch | Similar rhythm to single crochet |
| Purl `P` | The reverse of a knit stitch, creates texture | No direct crochet equivalent |
| Bind Off `BO` | Securing stitches to finish your work | Like finishing off and weaving in |
| Yarn Over `yo` | Wrapping yarn over the needle to create an eyelet | Same term, same idea |
| Stitch `st` | Each loop on the needle | Same as crochet |

## What are the basic knitting stitches?

If you are new to knitting, [YouTube knitting tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+tutorials) provide clear, helpful guidance.

### Slip knot

<Steps>
1. Loop the yarn, insert the needle through the loop, and pull it through to make a loop on the needle.
2. Pull gently on both yarn ends to tighten the knot against the needle.
</Steps>

### Long-tail cast on

<Steps>
1. Measure a tail about three times the width of your project. Make a slip knot at that point.
2. Place the slip knot on the needle. Hold the needle in your right hand.
3. Loop the yarn around your left thumb and index finger.
4. Insert the needle upward through the thumb loop.
5. Bring the needle over and through the index finger loop.
6. Drop the thumb loop and pull the yarn gently to tighten the stitch.
7. Repeat until you have the desired number of stitches.
</Steps>

### Knit stitch

<Steps>
1. Hold the needle with cast-on stitches in your left hand. Hold the empty needle in your right hand.
2. Insert the right needle upward through the first stitch, from front to back.
3. Wrap the yarn counter-clockwise around the right needle.
4. Pull the right needle through the stitch, bringing the wrapped yarn with it.
5. Slide the old stitch off the left needle. The new stitch is on the right needle.
</Steps>

### Purl stitch

<Steps>
1. Hold the needle with stitches in your left hand. Hold the empty needle in your right hand.
2. Insert the right needle downward through the first stitch, from back to front.
3. Wrap the yarn counter-clockwise around the right needle.
4. Pull the right needle through the stitch, bringing the wrapped yarn with it.
5. Slide the old stitch off the left needle. The new stitch is on the right needle.
</Steps>

### Bind off

<Steps>
1. Knit the first two stitches onto the right needle.
2. Insert the left needle into the first stitch you knit (the one farther from the tip).
3. Lift this stitch over the second stitch and off the right needle.
4. Knit one more stitch onto the right needle.
5. Repeat steps 2-4 until one stitch remains.
6. Cut the yarn, pull the tail through the last stitch, and tighten.
</Steps>
