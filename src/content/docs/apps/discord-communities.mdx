Discord is a chat platform organized into servers, each dedicated to a specific community or topic. For crocheters and knitters, Discord servers offer real-time conversation, stitch-alongs, WIP sharing, and direct access to designers and fellow makers. This guide covers how to find craft-focused Discord communities and how to participate.

**Sources:** [Discord](https://discord.com/)

## How do I get started on Discord?

Discord works on desktop, web, and mobile. You can join for free and participate in as many servers as you like.

- **Create an account.** Go to [discord.com](https://discord.com/) and sign up with your email. Pick a username, as this will be your handle across all servers.
- **Download the app.** Discord works in a browser, but the desktop and mobile apps offer better notifications and voice features.
- **Join a server.** You need an invite link to join a server. Most craft communities post invite links on their social media profiles or websites.

## What are the best crochet and knit Discord servers?

Many yarn craft communities have active Discord servers. Here are some of the most popular types:

- **General craft servers.** Large communities focused on all fiber arts, with channels for crochet, knitting, spinning, and dyeing. Good for finding a broad range of conversation and help.
- **Designer servers.** Many independent designers run their own Discord servers for their pattern testers, fans, and customers. These often include early access to new patterns, tester calls, and direct Q and A with the designer.
- **Technique-specific servers.** Dedicated communities for techniques like Tunisian crochet, mosaic crochet, and colorwork knitting. These are great for deep dives into a specific method.
- **CAL and KAL servers.** Crochet-along and knit-along servers form around specific projects. They provide a space to share progress, ask questions, and stay motivated.

To find current active servers, check the social media profiles of designers you follow, or search for "crochet Discord" or "knitting Discord" to find community directories.

## How do I find more Discord communities?

- **Disboard.** This is the largest public Discord server directory. Search for "crochet" or "knitting" to find active servers.
- **Designer links.** Designers often link their Discord servers in their Instagram, YouTube, or newsletter. If you enjoy a designer's patterns, check if they have a community.
- **Reddit and social media.** Crochet and knitting subreddits and Facebook groups sometimes have recommended server lists or invite threads.
- **Word of mouth.** Ask fellow crafters in other spaces if they know of active servers. The fiber arts community is connected, and recommendations are common.

## What is Discord etiquette in craft communities?

Every server has its own rules, but these guidelines apply in most craft spaces:

- **Read the rules channel first.** Servers usually post rules when you join. Read them before posting.
- **Use the right channels.** Servers organize conversation by topic (crochet help, finished objects, yarn talk). Posting in the correct channel keeps things tidy.
- **Share WIPs and FOs respectfully.** Many servers have dedicated channels for works in progress and finished objects. If you are selling items or promoting a pattern, check if there is a designated self-promotion channel.
- **Credit patterns and designers.** When sharing a photo of something you made, mention the pattern name and designer. This is standard practice and helps other crafters find the pattern.
- **Ask for help clearly.** When asking for troubleshooting advice, include what stitch or row you are on, what hook or needle size you are using, and a photo if possible. The more context you give, the better the help you will receive.

## What features does Discord offer for crafters?

Beyond basic chat, Discord has features that make it useful for yarn craft communities:

- **Voice channels.** Some servers run voice-based craft-alongs where members chat while they work. You can share your screen to show your progress or a tricky section.
- **Thread channels.** Threads keep side conversations from cluttering the main channel. You can ask a quick question in a thread without disrupting the main flow.
- **Bots.** Some servers use bots for pattern reminders, project tracking, or even stitch counters. Bots are optional but can add functionality.
- **Events.** Servers can schedule events like CAL start dates, live Q and A sessions with designers, or themed photo challenges. Events appear on the server calendar.
- **Roles.** Servers use roles to tag members by craft preference, skill level, or availability for testing. You can often assign yourself roles to see relevant channels.

## What tips and tricks should I know?

- **Start with one or two servers.** Joining too many at once can be overwhelming. Pick a general craft server and a designer server to start.
- **Mute channels you do not need.** You can mute individual channels or the whole server. This keeps notifications manageable.
- **Use the search bar.** Servers accumulate a lot of messages. Search for "beginner" or "yarn sub" to find past discussions before asking a question.
- **Introduce yourself.** Most servers have an introductions channel. Saying hello is a good way to break the ice and start connecting with other members.
- **Check for tester calls.** Many designers post tester calls in their Discord servers before announcing them publicly. Being active in server chats can give you early access to opportunities.
