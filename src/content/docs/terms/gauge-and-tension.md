**Gauge** tells you how many stitches and rows fit in an inch (or 4 inches). You measure it in a swatch. In the UK, many crafters call this "tension."

Gauge matters because it controls the size and fit of your finished project. Taking a few minutes to make a swatch, check it, and adjust your hook or needles can save you time, yarn, and frustration.

- **Stitch gauge** = number of stitches across a set width (usually 4 inches / 10 cm)
- **Row gauge** = number of rows down a set length (usually 4 inches / 10 cm)

**Sources:** [The Craft Yarn Council](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/)

## Why is gauge important?

Gauge ensures your finished item matches the pattern dimensions. Getting it right is critical for garments, amigurumi, and any fitted item.

- Ensures your finished item matches the pattern dimensions.
- Avoids projects that are too small or too big.
- Helps with consistency when using different yarns, hooks, or needles.
- Critical for garments, toys (amigurumi), and fitted items.


## How to check your gauge

Knit or crochet a 4"x4" (10x10 cm) swatch using your pattern yarn and hook or needles. Measure stitches and rows within that square, then adjust your hook or needle size if needed.

1. Use the yarn and hook or needles recommended in the pattern.
2. Work a sample swatch, usually about 4"x4" (10x10 cm), in the pattern stitch.
3. Lay your swatch flat (don't stretch).
4. Measure stitches and rows within the 4" square.
5. Adjust hook or needle size if your gauge is off:
   - Too many stitches = use larger hook or needles.
   - Too few stitches = use smaller hook or needles.


## Tips for consistent tension

Hold your yarn comfortably and practice even stitches. Using the same hook or needle type and size throughout helps maintain consistency.

- Hold your yarn comfortably, not too tight or loose.
- Practice stitches evenly.
- Use the same hook or needle type and size throughout.
- Avoid changing tension mid-project.
- Wash and block your swatch to see true size.


## Crochet gauge chart (hook sizes)

| Yarn Weight            | Recommended Hook Size (mm) | Gauge (Stitches x Rows per 4")              |
|-----------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Lace / Fingering**   | 1.5 – 2.25 mm              | 32 – 42 sts, 32 – 42 rows                   |
| **Super Fine / Sock**  | 2.25 – 3.5 mm              | 21 – 32 sts, 28 – 36 rows                   |
| **Fine / Sport**       | 3.5 – 4.5 mm               | 16 – 20 sts, 24 – 32 rows                   |
| **Light / DK**         | 4.5 – 5.5 mm               | 12 – 17 sts, 22 – 30 rows                   |
| **Medium / Worsted**   | 5.5 – 6.5 mm               | 11 – 14 sts, 18 – 24 rows                   |
| **Bulky / Chunky**     | 6.5 – 9 mm                 | 8 – 11 sts, 16 – 20 rows                    |
| **Super Bulky**        | 9 – 15 mm                  | 7 – 9 sts, 12 – 16 rows                     |
| **Jumbo / Roving**     | 15 mm and up               | 6 or fewer sts, 10 – 14 rows                |

## Quick gauge troubleshooting

| Problem                | Solution                           |
|------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Too many stitches      | Use bigger hook or needles         |
| Too few stitches       | Use smaller hook or needles        |
| Rows too tall or short | Adjust tension or hook/needle size |
| Inconsistent tension   | Practice and relax hands           |
