---
title: Which should you learn first, crochet or knitting?
description: A practical guide to help beginners decide whether to start with crochet or knitting, with what each craft is best for and what to expect
---

You want to start making things with yarn. Should you start with crochet or knitting? Both crafts use yarn and similar tools, but they produce different kinds of fabric and work at different speeds. This guide covers how they compare.

## Quick comparison

| | Crochet | Knitting |
|---|---|---|
| Tools | One hook | Two needles |
| Fabric feel | Thicker, denser, less stretch | Thinner, drapier, more stretch |
| Speed of visible progress | Faster (taller stitches) | Slower (shorter stitches) |
| Active stitches | One loop at a time | Many stitches on the needle |
| Fixing mistakes | Easy to pull out, no dropped stitches | Dropped stitches can unravel rows |
| Best for | Amigurumi, bags, hats, blankets, home decor | Garments, socks, sweaters, shawls, lace |
| Yarn usage | More yarn per project | Less yarn per project |
| Learning curve | Slightly easier to pick up | Slightly steeper start |

## Start with crochet if you want to make...

Crochet is ideal for projects that benefit from structure and stiffness.

**Amigurumi and toys.** Crochet creates firm, sculptural fabric that holds stuffing well. This is the easiest way to make stuffed animals and dolls by hand. The [amigurumi getting started guide](/guides/amigurumi-get-started/) covers everything you need.

**Bags, baskets, and home decor.** The dense fabric holds its shape without extra interfacing. This makes crochet the better choice for items that need to stand up on their own.

**Hats and blankets.** Crochet works up fast. A hat can take an evening. A blanket takes days, not weeks. Beginners see progress quickly, which helps maintain motivation.

**Projects with bold texture.** Crochet stitches sit on top of the fabric. Textured stitches are easy to see and feel. This makes crochet approachable for beginners who want visible results.

## Start with knitting if you want to make...

Knitting is the better choice for items that need to move with the body.

**Garments (sweaters, cardigans).** Knit fabric stretches and drapes. It follows the shape of the body in a way crochet cannot match. This is the number one reason to learn knitting.

**Socks.** Knitted socks stretch to fit the foot. Crochet socks are stiff and uncomfortable to wear inside shoes.

**Shawls and lace.** Knitted lace is lighter and more airy than crochet lace. The fabric flows better and drapes over the shoulders.

**Colorwork and cables.** Knit stitches are uniform and close together, making stranded colorwork and cable patterns crisp and detailed.

## Most people end up doing both

Many fiber artists start with one craft and pick up the other after a few months or years. Knowing both crafts gives you more options. You can choose the best tool for each project instead of forcing everything into one craft.

The [skills that transfer](/reference/skills-that-transfer/) page covers what carries over if you decide to learn the second craft.

## What matters more than which craft you choose

The biggest factor in sticking with a new craft is having a good experience at the start.

**Use good yarn.** Cheap acrylic yarn splits, feels unpleasant, and makes it harder to see your stitches. A smooth, light-colored, medium-weight yarn in a natural fiber or good quality blend makes learning easier. The [choosing yarn](/reference/choosing-yarn/) guide has recommendations for both crafts.

**Take a class or find a tutorial.** Having someone show you the basics in person or on video is faster than figuring it out from written instructions alone.

**Start with a simple project.** A washcloth, scarf, or small square teaches the basics without overwhelming you. Finishing something small builds confidence.

**Practice consistently.** Ten minutes a day is better than two hours once a week. Short sessions build muscle memory faster than long sessions.

Pick the craft that matches the projects you want to make. There is no wrong choice. Both crafts are enjoyable, portable, and rewarding.
