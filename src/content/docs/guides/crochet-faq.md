## What yarn should I use as a beginner?

Start with a smooth, light-colored, medium-weight yarn (worsted weight / category 4). Smooth yarn makes it easier to see your stitches, and light colors help you see the stitch definition. Avoid dark, fuzzy, or textured yarn until you are comfortable with the basics.

## What hook size should I start with?

A 5 mm (US H-8) hook paired with worsted weight yarn is a comfortable starting point. This combination is forgiving and easy to handle while you learn basic stitches.

## Why are my edges uneven?

Uneven edges usually happen when you accidentally add or skip stitches at the beginning or end of rows. Counting your stitches at the end of each row helps catch this early. Using a stitch marker in the first and last stitch can also help.

## Why does my crochet curl at the edges?

Curling is common with certain stitches and yarns. Blocking your finished piece usually helps. If curling happens while you work, try loosening your tension or using a larger hook.

## How do I count stitches?

Each upright V shape across the top of your work is one stitch. Count them after every row to make sure you have the right number. Place a stitch marker every 10 or 20 stitches to make counting easier on long rows.

## How do I change colors?

Work the last stitch of the old color until you have two loops left on the hook. Yarn over with the new color and pull through both loops to complete the stitch. Continue with the new color.

## How do I fix a mistake?

If the mistake is in the current row, you can undo stitches one at a time by pulling the hook out and gently tugging the yarn to unravel back to the error. For mistakes in earlier rows, you may need to rip out several rows. This is called frogging, and every crocheter does it.

## How do I read a crochet pattern?

Crochet patterns use standard abbreviations and symbols. Start by scanning the whole pattern, then read the abbreviation list. Work each row or round one step at a time. The [reading patterns](/reference/reading-patterns/) guide covers everything in detail.

## What does gauge mean and why does it matter?

Gauge tells you how many stitches and rows fit in a 4-inch square. Matching the pattern gauge ensures your project turns out the right size. Always make a gauge swatch before starting a garment or fitted item. See the [gauge and tension](/terms/gauge-and-tension/) guide for more.

## How do I keep my tension consistent?

Hold your yarn in a way that feels comfortable and allows the yarn to flow evenly through your fingers. Practice is the best way to develop consistent tension. Taking breaks and relaxing your hands also helps.
