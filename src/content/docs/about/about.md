Crochetly is a free, educational crochet and knitting resource, not a shop or marketplace. There are no products for sale, no subscriptions, and no downloads. Just clear instruction to help you learn and improve.

## About me

I'm Kim, known online as JesterKim.

I started Crochetly because I wanted a crochet reference site that was straightforward, ad-free, and focused entirely on teaching. No shops, no affiliate links, no fluff. Just clear explanations.

Crochet has been a creative outlet for me for years, and I wanted to share the techniques and knowledge I have picked up along the way. Over time, it became clear that many crocheters also knit, so Crochetly expanded to cover knitting too.

You can find me on [Ravelry](https://www.ravelry.com/people/JesterKim) and [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/jesterkimknits/).

## My knitting journey

### Knitting terms explained for crocheters

Coming from crochet, the first thing you'll notice is that knitting has two basic stitches (knit and purl) instead of crochet's single loop structure. A knit stitch looks like a V on the right side, and a purl stitch looks like a bump. Stockinette is the knitting equivalent of single crochet: the default fabric. "Right side" and "wrong side" matter more in knitting because stockinette looks very different on each side. A "yarn over" in knitting is not the same as in crochet. In crochet you yarn over with every stitch, but in knitting a yarn over is a deliberate increase that leaves a hole. "Casting on" replaces the foundation chain, and "binding off" replaces fastening off. Once you know these differences, most knitting pattern instructions become straightforward.

### What surprised me when learning to knit

It wasn't as hard as I thought it would be, especially given the amount of conversation about which craft is better, harder, or easier. Since I was already used to working with yarn, and I really like watching YouTube tutorials whenever I run into a challenge, I picked it up quickly. Taking a learn-to-knit class at my local yarn shop (plus a follow-up class for colorwork) made a huge difference.

### Crochet habits that don't work in knitting

Crochet doesn't build off past stitches the same way knitting does. In crochet, each row is largely independent of the row below it structurally. That means I can start a crochet project and figure things out as I go. Knitting doesn't work that way. Every stitch depends on the stitches below it, and if I try to wing it, I end up with mistakes that are hard to fix. I need a pattern for knitting, and I follow it carefully.

### Holding needles if you're used to hooks

I find holding knitting needles causes less hand fatigue than holding a crochet hook. The weight of the project hangs from the needles more naturally. But I like working with both, so I keep multiple projects going at once.

### Why knitting feels slower than crochet

Knitting often feels slower because I'm usually working with fingering-weight yarn on big projects like sweaters. Crochet works up faster even with the same yarn weight because the stitches are taller. But the difference is mostly about the project and yarn choice.

### Beginner knitting mistakes I made

Most of my early mistakes were around garment construction. I didn't always understand how shaping, fit, and fabric choices would affect the final piece. Over time, I learned to modify patterns so the outcome matches what I want. That's one of the reasons I started this site -- to capture those tips and notes for myself and for anyone else learning both crafts.
