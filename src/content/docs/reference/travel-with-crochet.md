title: Travel checklist
description: Packing checklist for bringing knitting or crochet on trips, including essential tools, TSA-friendly supplies, project-planning tips, and what to pack in your travel craft kit.
---

A knitting or crochet project is a great travel buddy. Next time you plan a trip, use this checklist to pack your project.

## What project should you take on a trip?

Choose a project that works well away from home. The best travel projects are simple, portable, and easy to pick up after interruptions.

- Pick a **simple pattern** you know well enough to work without checking every row.
- Avoid projects that need frequent counting, complex charts, or many tools.
- Socks, hats, scarves, shawls, and dishcloths are good choices. Large blankets and complex lace are not.
- Test the pattern at home before you leave so you know the stitch rhythm.
- A project worked in rounds is easier to manage on a plane than a large flat piece.
- Bring printed instructions or screenshots in case you have no signal.

## What should you pack in a travel craft kit?

Pack a zippered pouch or clear bag, knitting needles or crochet hooks (plastic or bamboo for flights), small scissors or thread cutter, enough yarn for the whole trip, tapestry needle, stitch markers, and a printed or offline pattern.

### Essentials
- A zippered pouch or a TSA-compliant clear bag
- Knitting needles or crochet hooks (preferably plastic or bamboo for flights)
- Small yarn scissors or thread cutter
- Yarn (enough for the whole trip)
- Tapestry needle (for weaving in ends)
- Stitch markers
- Pattern (printed or downloaded offline)
- **Lifeline.** A length of scrap yarn or smooth thread to thread through your stitches in case you need to remove your needles or hooks at security.
- **Cable stoppers.** If you use interchangeable needles, these let you detach the tips and secure your stitches.

### Optional
- Row counter
- Measuring tape
- Project bag or pouch
- Notebook and pen
- Foldable empty bag (for yarn purchases at your destination)

## What TSA and airport rules apply to knitting and crochet supplies?

Policies vary by country, airport, and even the security officer on duty. Here is what to expect in common regions.

- **United States (TSA):** Knitting needles and crochet hooks are allowed in carry-on bags. Scissors under 4 inches from the pivot are permitted.
- **European Union:** Knitting needles and crochet hooks are generally allowed under EU regulations. Some airlines (Air France, KLM) prohibit them even when the local law permits. British Airways explicitly allows them.
- **Country-specific issues:** Departure airports in Istanbul (Turkey), Cancun (Mexico), Fiji, Greece, and various points in Southeast Asia are known to confiscate needles even when the airline allows them. Always research both your departing and arriving airport.
- **The final say:** Security officers can override written policies. A metal needle that passes in one city may be confiscated in the next, even on the same airline.

Always check the regulations for both your departing and arriving airports before you fly.

## Should you take metal or wooden tools?

Wooden, bamboo, or plastic needles and hooks are safer for carry-on bags. They do not trigger metal detectors as prominently and look less threatening on security scans.

If you prefer metal tools (for speed or feel), bring an interchangeable set with cable stoppers. If an officer questions your needles, you can remove the tips, put stoppers on the cable, and keep your project intact. The cable alone on the scan is unlikely to raise concerns.

**Backup plan:** Keep spare tips or a spare set in your checked luggage. If your carry-on tools are confiscated, you can pick up your project fresh from the spare set on arrival.

## What about scissors and other tools?

Keep your tools organized in a clear pouch so you can pull them out quickly at security without unpacking your whole bag.

- **Scissors:** TSA allows scissors under 4 inches from the pivot. EU allows scissors under 6 cm (2.3 inches). Rounded-tip children's scissors look less threatening. A thread cutter pendant or nail clippers are reliable alternatives.
- **Tapestry needles:** Blunt tapestry needles are generally allowed, but very sharp pointed needles may face scrutiny.
- **Stitch markers, row counters, measuring tape:** No issues. Keep them in your clear pouch.

## How do you pack enough yarn without overpacking?

Yarn takes up space and weight. Packing the right amount saves headaches.

- Weigh or measure your yarn before you leave. Count how many grams one row uses, then multiply by the number of rows you expect to work.
- Bring 20–30% extra in case you have more free time than planned or need to rip back.
- Wind yarn into center-pull balls or cakes at home. Loose skeins tangle and take up more room.
- If your pattern uses more than one color, wind each into its own ball and label them.

## How do you protect your yarn and project?

- Keep your project in a bag that closes fully. Sand, dust, and humidity can damage yarn.
- A ziplock bag inside your project bag adds spill protection inside your carry-on.
- If you are traveling to a humid or rainy destination, avoid bringing projects in natural fibers (wool, alpaca, silk) that absorb moisture. Acrylic and cotton blends handle travel better.
- If you buy yarn at your destination, store it separately from your project to avoid mixing dye lots.

## How do you find yarn shops at your destination?

- Use Google Maps and Ravelry's yarn shop database to find stores before you go.
- Call ahead or check websites for hours. Small yarn shops often have irregular schedules.
- Ask at local bookstores, hotels, or craft groups for recommendations.
- Look for mills, farms, or studios that welcome visitors. Some require appointments.
- **Pack an empty foldable bag.** Yarn purchases can fill your luggage faster than you expect.

## What happens if security confiscates your tools?

Stay calm and polite. Arguing will not change the officer's decision.

- If you can, ask whether you can go back to check your bag instead of surrendering the tools.
- If you cannot, **put a lifeline through your stitches** before handing over your needles or hooks. Thread a length of scrap yarn or smooth thread through every live stitch on the needle or hook. Remove the tool. Your project stays intact, and you can pick it up with new tools at your destination.
- If you have interchangeable needles with cable stoppers, you may only need to surrender the tips. Ask the officer.

## Can you knit or crochet on the plane?

Most airlines allow knitting and crochet on board, but use common sense.

- Stop during **takeoff and landing** (the same times electronics must be stowed).
- Stop during **turbulence**. Loose needles in a bumpy cabin are a safety hazard.
- Be mindful of your elbow space. Large projects with long cables or wide fabric can spill into your neighbor's seat.
- Stop during meal service or tuck your project away so the flight attendant can serve without navigating around your yarn.
- If you are unsure about your airline's policy, contact customer service before your flight.

## Etiquette tips for flying with your craft

- Keep your project small and contained. A pair of socks or a hat takes up almost no space. A queen-size blanket does not.
- Avoid clicking needles during quiet periods or dark cabin hours.
- If your seatmate asks about your project, show it briefly and gauge their interest. Some people enjoy the conversation. Others just want to sleep.
- Offer to stop or switch to a different activity if your neighbor seems disturbed. Not everyone loves knitting, and that is fine.
