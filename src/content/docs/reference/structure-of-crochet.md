---
title: Structure of crochet
description: What is a crochet fabric, how it differs from woven and knitted fabric, the anatomy of stitches and rows, how stitch height works, and why crochet is denser and less elastic than knitting
---

Crochet creates fabric from a single continuous yarn using a hook to pull loops through other loops. Unlike knitting, only one stitch is live at a time. The rest are locked in place. This gives crochet a denser, firmer fabric with less stretch.

## How is crochet fabric different from woven and knitted fabric?

Woven fabric uses two perpendicular yarn sets (warp and weft) for firm, non-stretchy cloth. Knitted fabric uses interconnected loops on needles for elastic fabric with many live stitches. Crochet fabric uses a hook to pull loops through completed stitches. Only one live loop at a time.

- **Woven fabric:** Two perpendicular yarn sets, firm, minimal stretch.
- **Knitted fabric:** Interconnected loops, many live stitches, very elastic.
- **Crochet fabric:** Interconnected loops, one live stitch at a time, dense and firm, less elastic than knitting but holds shape well.

## What is a stitch?

A stitch is a loop of yarn pulled through another loop using a hook. Once a stitch is completed, it is locked in place by the next stitch. This is why crochet does not unravel as easily as knitting. There are no live stitches waiting on a needle.

## What is a chain?

A chain is a series of loops pulled through one another to form a foundation row. Most crochet projects start with a chain, then stitches are worked into it. The chain is the crochet equivalent of a cast-on row in knitting.

## What is a row?

A row is a horizontal sequence of stitches worked across the piece. At the end of each row, you turn the work and chain one or more stitches (the turning chain) to bring the hook up to the height of the next row.

## What is the first row called?

The first row of stitches worked into the foundation chain does not have a special name. It is simply called the first row. Together, the foundation chain plus the first row of stitches form the starting edge.

## What is the last row called?

The last step is to **fasten off** or **finish off**. Cut the yarn and pull the tail through the last loop on the hook. This locks the stitch so the fabric cannot unravel.

## What is the shape of a crochet stitch?

A crochet stitch is closer to **square** than a knit stitch. Its width and height are more balanced. This affects gauge: stitch gauge and row gauge are often closer to each other than in knitting (for example, 14 stitches and 16 rows per 4 inches for worsted weight).

## How do basic crochet stitches differ in height?

Basic crochet stitches build height by adding yarn overs before inserting the hook. More yarn overs create taller stitches.

| Stitch | Abbreviation | Height | Yarn overs before hook insert |
|--------|-------------|--------|-------------------------------|
| Slip stitch | sl st | Shortest | 0 |
| Single crochet | sc | Short | 0 |
| Half double crochet | hdc | Medium | 1 |
| Double crochet | dc | Tall | 1 |
| Treble crochet | tr | Taller | 2 |

## How does crochet structure differ from knitting?

- **Live stitches:** Crochet has one live stitch at a time. Knitting holds many live stitches on needles.
- **Density:** Crochet fabric is denser and thicker than knitting with the same yarn.
- **Elasticity:** Crochet fabric has less stretch than knitted fabric.
- **Ripping back:** Crochet is easier to rip back. You just pull the yarn and the loops release. Knitting requires carefully picking up dropped stitches.

## What happens if you drop a crochet stitch?

Unlike knitting (where a dropped stitch can unravel a column of loops), dropping a crochet stitch usually affects only one stitch. The surrounding stitches lock it in place. You can often fix it by working back to the spot or using the yarn tail to close the gap.
