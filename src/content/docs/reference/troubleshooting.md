Crochet is a relaxing craft, but sometimes things go wrong. This guide covers common problems and how to fix them.

## How do you fix general crochet problems?

Most common issues have simple fixes. Adjust your hook size, slow down, count stitches, and block your finished pieces.

### Tension too tight or loose
Adjust your hook size or consciously relax your hand to even out stitches.

### Splitting yarn
Use a smoother yarn or a hook with a rounded tip. Slow down and watch your stitches carefully.

### Skipping stitches
Count your stitches regularly to avoid accidentally missing or adding stitches.

### Uneven edges
Make sure to chain the correct number of stitches at the beginning of rows and count your stitches.

### Curling edges
Try blocking your finished piece or use a larger hook for looser stitches.

### Confusing patterns
Use stitch markers and take notes.

## How do you correct crochet errors?

### Fixing a dropped stitch
When a stitch slips off the hook, insert the hook back into the loop and tighten. If it has started to unravel, catch the loose strand and pull it through to reform the stitch.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+dropped+stitch)

### Fixing a missed stitch
If you accidentally skip a stitch, you can work back to the gap or use a needle and yarn to fill in the missing stitch after finishing.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+missed+stitch)

### Fixing an accidental increase
Working two stitches in one space adds an extra stitch. To fix, skip one stitch on the next row to decrease back to the correct count.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+accidental+increase)

### Fixing an accidental decrease
Skipping a stitch removes one. To fix, work two stitches in one space on the next row to add the missing stitch back.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+accidental+decrease)

### Fixing a running stitch
When a stitch starts unraveling down the row, catch the loose loop with your hook and pull the working yarn through to reform the stitch before it runs further.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+running+stitch)

### Fixing a twisted foundation chain
If your foundation chain is twisted before joining in the round, the whole piece will be off. Remove the hook, straighten the chain, and reinsert before joining.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+twisted+foundation+chain)

### Fixing a tight foundation chain
If the starting chain is too tight, redo it with a hook one or two sizes larger, then switch back to the recommended hook for the first row.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+tight+foundation+chain)

### Fixing wavy or ruffled edges
Wavy edges usually mean too many stitches. Count your stitches and frog back to the row where the count went wrong, then rework with fewer stitches.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+wavy+edges)

### Fixing puckered fabric
Puckered fabric means too few stitches. Count your stitches and add increases in the next row or frog back to correct the count.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+puckered+fabric)

### Unraveling stitch by stitch (tinking)
To undo one stitch at a time, gently pull the working yarn to loosen the last stitch, then pull the loop out of the stitch. Insert the hook back into the previous stitch before continuing.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+tinking+unravel+stitch+by+stitch)

### Unraveling entire rows (frogging)
To rip out multiple rows, pull out the hook, gently pull the working yarn to unravel back to the error, then carefully reinsert the hook into the last loop.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+frogging+unravel+rows)

### Adding lifelines
Thread a strand of scrap yarn through the loops of a completed row using a tapestry needle. If you make a mistake later, you can rip back safely to the lifeline without losing your place.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+add+lifeline)

### Weaving in ends (darning)
Use a tapestry needle to weave yarn tails back through the stitches on the wrong side. Follow a zigzag path so the ends stay secure and invisible.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+weave+in+ends)

### Fixing color changes
For a cleaner color transition, work the last yarn over of the old color with the new color, then pull the new color through to complete the stitch.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+color+changes)

### Repairing crochet fabric (mending)
Stitch a hole closed using a tapestry needle and matching yarn, following the path of the surrounding stitches to match the fabric.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+mending+hole+repair)

### Fixing a loose magic ring
If the magic ring is loose, pull the tail to tighten it. If it has come undone completely, re-do the ring and rework the first round.
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+fix+loose+magic+ring)

## How do you fix common amigurumi problems?

Use a smaller hook than the yarn label recommends. Tight stitches keep stuffing from showing through. Keep your tension consistent and use stitch markers to track rounds.

### Holes between stitches
Use a smaller hook than recommended. Tight stitches prevent stuffing from showing.

### Lopsided shapes
Check increases and decreases carefully and count stitches each round.

### Stuffing shows or lumps
Stuff firmly but not too much; use polyester fiberfill and smooth it evenly.

### Safety eyes slipping
Secure with washers or embroider eyes for safety, especially for children's toys.

### Working in spirals without joining
Use a stitch marker to keep track of the beginning of each round.

## How do you fix Tunisian crochet problems?

Use a smooth hook and yarn that glides easily. Block your projects and experiment with hook size. Use stitch markers to count stitches on the forward pass.

### Hard to pull loops over
Make sure your hook is smooth and use a yarn that glides easily.

### Rows curling or fabric stiff
Try blocking your project; also, experiment with hook size or yarn weight.

### Losing loops off the hook
Keep a firm grip on the hook and consider using a hook with a stopper or a clip.

### Counting stitches on the forward pass
Use stitch markers or place a marker every 10 stitches to keep track.

### Inconsistent tension between forward and return pass
Practice keeping even tension on both passes for uniform fabric.

## What are the bonus tips for troubleshooting?

Use stitch markers, count stitches often, watch tutorials, take breaks when your hands get tired, and be patient with yourself.

- **Use stitch markers** to keep track of your place.
- **Count stitches often** so you can catch mistakes early.
- **Watch tutorials or ask for help** in crochet communities.
- **Take breaks** when your hands get tired. Fatigue leads to mistakes.
- **Be patient with yourself.** Mistakes are part of learning.
