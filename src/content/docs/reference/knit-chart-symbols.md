---
title: Knit chart symbols
description: Quick reference for standard knit chart symbols, including knit and purl stitches, increases, decreases, cables, lace, and chart reading tips.
---

Knitting patterns use charts to show stitch patterns visually. Each square in a chart represents one stitch, and symbols within the square tell you what to do with that stitch. Here are the standard chart symbols used by designers and publishers.

**Source:** [Craft Yarn Council](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/standards) knit chart symbol standards

## What are the standard knit chart symbols?

Charts are read from bottom to top. The right side (odd rows) is worked from right to left, and the wrong side (even rows) is worked from left to right. Each symbol represents one stitch or a group of stitches.

### Basic stitches

| Symbol | Stitch Name | Worked on right side | Worked on wrong side |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Empty square or \| | Knit (k) | Knit | Purl |
| - or horizontal line | Purl (p) | Purl | Knit |
| • or small dot | Garter stitch | Knit | Knit |
| / or /- | Slip stitch (sl) | Slip purlwise with yarn in back | Slip purlwise with yarn in front |
| No symbol | No stitch | Skip this square | Skip this square |

### Increases

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
|--------|------|---------|
| O | Yarnover (yo) | Wrap yarn around needle to create a new stitch |
| M or ^ | Make one (M1) | Pick up bar between stitches and knit through front or back loop |
| / (diagonal up) | Kfb | Knit through front and back loop of same stitch |
| V | Knit into stitch below (k1b) | Knit into the stitch below the next stitch |

### Decreases

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
|--------|------|---------|
| / | K2tog | Knit two stitches together (right leaning) |
| \\ | Ssk | Slip slip knit (left leaning) |
| / with 3 | K3tog | Knit three stitches together |
| \\ with 3 | Sk2p | Slip 1, knit 2 together, pass slipped stitch over |
| / through | P2tog | Purl two stitches together |

### Cable symbols

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
|--------|------|---------|
| / over \ | C4B or C6B | Cable back: slip stitches to cable needle and hold in back, knit next stitches, then knit from cable needle |
| \ over / | C4F or C6F | Cable front: slip stitches to cable needle and hold in front, knit next stitches, then knit from cable needle |
| / with dot | T4B | Twist back: slip stitches to cable needle and hold in back, knit next, then purl from cable needle |
| \ with dot | T4F | Twist front: slip stitches to cable needle and hold in front, purl next, then knit from cable needle |

### Lace symbols

| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
|--------|------|---------|
| O | Yarnover (yo) | Yarn over creates a hole, matched by a decrease elsewhere |
| / \ | Central double decrease (sk2p or cd) | Slip 1, k2tog, pass slipped stitch over |
| / | K2tog | Right leaning decrease paired with a yarnover |
| \\ | Ssk | Left leaning decrease paired with a yarnover |

## How do I read a cable chart?

Cable charts show the cable cross explicitly. The symbols tell you which stitches cross in front of or behind the other stitches. The number of stitches in the cable determines the symbol. A C4B means 4 stitches total: 2 slipped to the back, 2 knit from the left needle, then 2 knit from the cable needle.

## How do I read a lace chart?

Lace charts pair every yarnover with a decrease to keep the stitch count constant. If you see a yarnover on one row, check the same row for a matching decrease. The decrease may appear before or after the yarnover depending on whether it leans right or left.

## What if the chart uses different symbols?

Designers and publishers use slight variations on these symbols. Always check the chart key before starting. The key explains exactly what each symbol means for that specific pattern. Some charts include a written key next to the chart or in a separate section.

## How do I check a chart is correct?

Count the stitches on each chart row and verify they match the stitch count in the written instructions. If the chart and written directions disagree, test both on a small swatch or check with the designer. Most chart errors appear as missing or extra symbols in a single row.
