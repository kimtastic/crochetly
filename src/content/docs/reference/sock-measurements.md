Use this guide to find standard measurements for socks. Sock sizing varies more than other garments because fit depends on foot length, circumference, and personal preference for snugness.

## What are the standard sock sizes?

Sock sizes range from child (3-4 in foot length) to adult extra large (12-13 in). Foot circumference and leg length also vary by size.

| Size | Foot Circumference | Foot Length | Leg Length | Notes |
|------|--------------------|-------------|------------|-------|
| Child (0-12 mo) | 4-4.5 in (10-11 cm) | 3-4.5 in (7.5-11.5 cm) | 3-5 in (7.5-12.5 cm) | Short leg for diaper cover fit |
| Child (1-3 yr) | 5-5.5 in (12.5-14 cm) | 5-6 in (12.5-15 cm) | 4-6 in (10-15 cm) | |
| Child (4-6 yr) | 5.5-6 in (14-15 cm) | 6-7 in (15-18 cm) | 5-7 in (12.5-18 cm) | |
| Child (7-10 yr) | 6-6.5 in (15-16.5 cm) | 7-8 in (18-20 cm) | 6-8 in (15-20 cm) | |
| Youth | 6.5-7.5 in (16.5-19 cm) | 8-9 in (20-23 cm) | 6-9 in (15-23 cm) | |
| Adult S (Women's 5-7) | 7.5-8 in (19-20 cm) | 9-9.5 in (23-24 cm) | 7-10 in (18-25 cm) | |
| Adult M (Women's 8-10 / Men's 6-8) | 8-8.5 in (20-21.5 cm) | 9.5-10 in (24-25.5 cm) | 8-12 in (20-30 cm) | Most common size |
| Adult L (Women's 11+ / Men's 9-11) | 8.5-9.5 in (21.5-24 cm) | 10-11 in (25.5-28 cm) | 8-14 in (20-35 cm) | |
| Adult XL (Men's 12-14) | 9.5-11 in (24-28 cm) | 11-13 in (28-33 cm) | 8-14 in (20-35 cm) | |

For a snug fit, design sock circumference with 10-15% negative ease. A sock that measures 8 in (20 cm) around fits a foot with a 9 in (23 cm) circumference.

## What ease works best for socks?

Socks need negative ease to stay up and feel comfortable inside shoes.

- **Cuff-down construction:** 10-15% negative ease at the foot. The leg can have slightly less negative ease for comfort.
- **Toe-up construction:** Same negative ease targets, but you can try the sock on as you knit to fine-tune fit.
- **Ribbing:** Stretches more than stockinette, so ribbed socks need less negative ease than stockinette socks. A ribbed cuff can have 15-20% negative ease for grip.
- **Sock yarn weight:** Fingering weight socks (the most common) need the full negative ease. Heavier sock yarns (sport, DK) may need slightly less because the fabric is thicker.

## How do I measure foot length for socks?

The sock foot should be 0.5-0.75 in (1-2 cm) shorter than the actual foot. This negative ease keeps the sock snug and prevents bunching inside shoes.

To measure: Stand with your foot flat on a piece of paper. Mark the tip of the longest toe and the back of the heel. Measure the distance between the two marks.

For a good fit, knit the sock foot to your desired length minus 0.5-0.75 in (1-2 cm). The toe decrease and Kitchener stitch will add the remaining length.

## What are the standard heel measurements?

Heel depth varies by size and construction method.

| Size | Heel Flap Depth | Heel Turn Depth |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Child | 1-1.5 in (2.5-4 cm) | 0.5-1 in (1.5-2.5 cm) |
| Youth | 1.5-1.75 in (4-4.5 cm) | 1-1.25 in (2.5-3 cm) |
| Adult S | 1.75-2 in (4.5-5 cm) | 1.25 in (3 cm) |
| Adult M | 2-2.25 in (5-5.5 cm) | 1.25-1.5 in (3-4 cm) |
| Adult L | 2.25-2.5 in (5.5-6.5 cm) | 1.5 in (4 cm) |
| Adult XL | 2.5-3 in (6.5-7.5 cm) | 1.5-1.75 in (4-4.5 cm) |

## What general tips apply to sock sizing?

Sock fit is personal. Swatch in the round and block your swatch before measuring. Adjust stitch count by multiples of the heel construction number (often 4) to maintain the heel pattern.

- **Swatch in the round** for accurate gauge. Purl tension differs from knit tension.
- **Block your swatch** before measuring. Sock yarns relax after washing.
- **Stitch count** should be divisible by the heel construction number (often 4 for a standard heel flap).
- **Toe shape** affects fit: a wedge toe is roomier, a star toe is more tapered.
- **Heel type** affects fit: heel flap and gusset offers the most adjustability; short-row heels are faster but less customizable.
