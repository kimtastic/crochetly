Wraps per inch (WPI) measures how many times a strand of yarn wraps around a ruler in one inch. It is a simple way to identify the weight of yarn when the label is gone. You hold the yarn, wrap it around a flat surface, and count the wraps.

**Source:** [Craft Yarn Council](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/standards) WPI standards

## How do I measure WPI?

You need a ruler or WPI tool and the yarn you want to identify.

1. Hold the ruler flat with the edge facing up.
2. Wrap the yarn around the ruler, keeping the strands close together but not overlapping or pulled tight.
3. Wrap over a 2 to 4 inch section, not just 1 inch, for a more accurate average.
4. Count how many wraps fit in 1 inch at the center of your wrapped section.

Do not pull the yarn tight. The wraps should sit next to each other without compressing. If the yarn is crimped or stretched, let it relax before measuring.

## What are the WPI ranges for each yarn weight?

Each CYC weight category has a standard WPI range. Match your measured WPI to the closest category.

| CYC Category | Yarn Weight | WPI Range |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Lace | 16+ WPI |
| 1 | Super Fine / Fingering | 14-16 WPI |
| 2 | Fine / Sport | 12-14 WPI |
| 3 | Light / DK | 11-13 WPI |
| 4 | Medium / Worsted | 9-11 WPI |
| 5 | Bulky / Chunky | 7-9 WPI |
| 6 | Super Bulky | 5-7 WPI |
| 7 | Jumbo | 4 or fewer WPI |

These ranges are guidelines, not strict boundaries. A yarn that wraps at 10 WPI is almost certainly a worsted weight, but a yarn at 11 WPI could be either a light worsted or a heavy DK. Use WPI alongside gauge and yardage information to confirm the weight.

## What if my yarn measures between two categories?

If your yarn falls between two WPI ranges, swatch it. Work a 4 by 4 inch swatch with the expected hook or needle size and compare the gauge to the standard ranges for each category. The gauge test will tell you which category fits better. Alternatively, check the yardage per 100g if you can measure it.

## Does WPI work for all yarn types?

WPI works best for smooth or lightly textured yarns. Heavy textures, boucle, eyelash, and ribbon yarns do not wrap cleanly and will give an inconsistent reading. For these yarns, use the recommended tool size on the label or swatch to determine the appropriate weight category.

## How can I use WPI in my patterns?

If you are writing a pattern that allows multiple yarns or substitutions, include the WPI range alongside the standard weight category. This helps makers who want to use yarn from their stash without a label. You can also use WPI to describe the yarn thickness in pattern notes or blog posts.
