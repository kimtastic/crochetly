---
title: Joining yarn
description: How to join a new ball of yarn in crochet and knitting, covering the weave as you go method, felted splice, Russian join, and magic knot with their pros, cons, and best-use scenarios
---

There is no single best way to join a new ball of yarn. The right method depends on your fiber type, the project, and whether the join will be visible. Some methods are invisible, some are fast, and some only work with certain yarns.

## Weave as you go

The simplest approach. Lay the old tail and the new yarn along your work, then stitch over both for several stitches. Drop the old tail and continue with the new yarn. Trim the tails after a few more rows.

Works with any yarn and both crafts. No prep work needed. The tails must be long enough to stay secured and may need weaving in later.

[Search YouTube for "weave as you go join yarn" tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=weave+as+you+go+join+yarn)

## Felted splice (spit splice)

Overlap the ends of the old and new yarn by a few inches. Wet them with water or saliva, then rub the overlapped section between your palms until the fibers fuse together. Allow to dry before continuing.

Only works with animal fibers (wool, alpaca, yak) that have scales that can felt. Invisible and leaves no tails to weave. Does not work with superwash (non-felting) wool, cotton, acrylic, or plant fibers.

[Search YouTube for "felted splice spit splice" tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+felted+splice+spit+splice)

## Russian join

Thread the old yarn onto a tapestry needle and weave it through the plies of the new yarn for about an inch, then do the same in reverse with the new yarn into the old. Pull tight and trim.

Works best with plied yarns. Creates a strong, secure join with no ends to weave. Takes practice and is harder with single-ply, very slippery, or thick yarns.

[Search YouTube for "Russian join" tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=russian+join+yarn)

## Magic knot

Overlap the old and new yarn, tie one overhand knot with each tail around the opposite strand, pull tight so the knots slide together, then tug firmly. Trim the tails close to the knot.

Works with any fiber and both crafts. Very fast and secure. The knot can be visible and may pull through loosely woven fabric. With very slippery yarns, the knot can come undone under tension.

[Search YouTube for "magic knot join yarn" tutorials](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=magic+knot+join+yarn)
