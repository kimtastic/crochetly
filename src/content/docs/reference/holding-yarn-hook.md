---
title: Holding yarn and a hook
description: Guide to holding a crochet hook and tensioning yarn, covering pencil grip vs knife grip, yarn tension methods, and tips for finding a comfortable hold that prevents hand fatigue
---

Crochet uses two main hook grips and several yarn tensioning methods. No single way is correct. The best hold is whatever feels comfortable and lets you crochet without pain.

## How do you hold a crochet hook?

There are two main ways to hold a crochet hook: the **pencil grip** and the **knife grip**. Both work for any stitch, yarn, or project. Try both and see which feels more natural.

### Pencil grip

Hold the hook like you would hold a pencil. Your thumb and index finger rest on the thumb rest, and the handle rests against the web of your hand.

- Hand is **over the hook**.
- Good **fine control** for detailed work (lace, amigurumi, thread crochet).
- Common among beginners and people with a sewing or drawing background.
- May cause hand fatigue in some crocheters over long sessions.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+pencil+grip)

### Knife grip

Hold the hook like you would hold a dinner knife. Your palm rests on top of the hook, and your thumb and fingers wrap around the handle.

- Hand is **on top of the hook**.
- More **power and leverage** for thicker yarns and tight tension.
- Less wrist strain for many experienced crocheters.
- Often preferred for long projects and bulky yarn.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=crochet+knife+grip)

## How do you tension the yarn?

The yarn passes through your non-hook hand (or your hook hand, depending on preference) to control tension. Tension affects stitch size and consistency. Here are the three most common methods.

### Over the index finger

Drape the yarn over your index finger and let it run through your palm. Hold the work between your thumb and middle finger.

- Most common method.
- Easy to adjust tension by raising or lowering your index finger.
- Good for beginners.

### Around the pinky

Wrap the yarn once around your pinky, then run it over your index finger.

- More tension control than the over-the-finger method.
- The pinky wrap creates friction that keeps the yarn from slipping.
- Good for crocheters who struggle with loose, uneven tension.

### Between the fingers

Thread the yarn between your ring and middle fingers, then over your index finger.

- Moderate tension, between the first two methods.
- Less common but preferred by some crocheters who find the pinky wrap too tight.

## How do you hold the yarn tail?

The short yarn tail (from your slip knot) is usually held between the middle finger and thumb of your hook hand, or between your pinky and palm. This keeps the foundation steady while you work the first row of stitches.

## Should you use a tension ring?

Tension rings (yarn guides that fit on your finger) are optional. They help some crocheters keep even tension without gripping the yarn tightly. They are not necessary but can help if you struggle with hand cramping or inconsistent tension.

## Tips for finding your hold

- Try both pencil and knife grips for at least 15 minutes each before deciding.
- Tension consistency matters more than grip style. Uneven stitches usually come from inconsistent tension, not from the wrong hold.
- Comfort is your main signal. Switch grips if your hand cramps or your wrist hurts.
- Your tension method can change with the project. Many crocheters switch between methods depending on the yarn weight and stitch pattern.
- Relax your shoulders and hands. White-knuckling the hook creates tension problems.
