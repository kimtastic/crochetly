title: Kitchener stitch (grafting)
description: Learn how to graft two sets of live stitches together using the Kitchener stitch, a seamless joining method for sock toes, shoulder seams, and mitten tops
---

**Grafting**, also called the Kitchener stitch, joins two sets of live stitches together seamlessly. It is commonly used for closing sock toes, shoulder seams, and the tops of mittens. The result looks like a continuous row of knitting with no visible seam.

This tutorial is adapted from the [Tin Can Knits grafting tutorial](https://blog.tincanknits.com/2013/10/03/kitchener-stitch/).

## Setup

1. Place your stitches on two needles with the wrong sides facing each other.
2. Thread your yarn tail or a new length of yarn onto a darning needle.
3. Insert the darning needle through the **first stitch on the front needle** as if to purl and pull the yarn through, leaving the stitch on the needle.
4. Insert the darning needle through the **first stitch on the back needle** as if to knit and pull the yarn through, leaving the stitch on the needle.

## Repeated steps

Repeat these four steps until one stitch remains on each needle:

1. Insert the darning needle through the **first stitch on the front needle knitwise**, pull the yarn through, and **slip that stitch off** the needle.
2. Insert the darning needle through the **next stitch on the front needle purlwise**, pull the yarn through, and **leave it on** the needle.
3. Insert the darning needle through the **first stitch on the back needle purlwise**, pull the yarn through, and **slip that stitch off** the needle.
4. Insert the darning needle through the **next stitch on the back needle knitwise**, pull the yarn through, and **leave it on** the needle.

When one stitch remains on each needle, work steps 1 and 3 once more. Break the yarn and move the tail to the inside of the work. Weave in your ends.

## Tips

- **Chant to stay on track:** front knit off / front purl on / back purl off / back knit on
- Keep a handwritten cue card with the steps in your knitting kit for quick reference.
- After threading through all stitches, use a blunt needle tip to gently tighten each stitch one at a time before weaving in ends. This makes the graft look like the rest of your knitting.
- Grafting works best on stockinette stitch. Grafting ribbing or purl stitches is possible but more advanced.
