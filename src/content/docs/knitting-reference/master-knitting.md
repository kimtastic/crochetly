The Knitting Guild Association (TKGA) Master Hand Knitting Program is the standard for advanced knitting certification in the United States. It is an educational program that tests your skills through swatches, reports, questions, and projects. It is not a competition or a course. It is an evaluation of your knitting knowledge and technique.

**Pricing note:** Costs listed on this page are as of July 2026. Check the TKGA site for current pricing.

**Source:** [The Knitting Guild Association](https://tkga.org/certification/master-hand-knitting/)

## What is the TKGA Master Hand Knitting Program?

The Master Hand Knitting Program was announced in 1987 as a way for TKGA members to have their knitting evaluated against specific criteria. It has three levels, each building on the techniques from the level before it. You work independently through each level, completing swatches, written work, and projects. A committee evaluates your submission and provides feedback.

You must pass each level before moving to the next. After passing Level 3, you earn the title of Master Knitter and receive a certificate and pin.

## Who should become a master knitter?

The program is for knitters who want to deepen their skills and demonstrate their mastery. You should consider it if:

- You have been knitting for several years and want to move past following patterns
- You want a structured way to improve your technique and fix bad habits
- You are interested in teaching, pattern writing, or tech editing
- You want formal recognition of your skills
- You enjoy working independently on challenging, self-guided projects

This is not a program for brand new knitters. TKGA offers correspondence courses for building basic skills before enrolling in the master program.

## What does Level 1 require?

Level 1 is the foundation. You learn to evaluate your own work at a professional level.

- **Cost:** $150
- **Time limit:** 12 months
- **Requirements:** Report on blocking, 18 swatches, 4 gauge worksheets, 23 questions, 1 mitten project, references

The swatches cover basic techniques and help you practice consistent tension and accurate execution. The written questions test your understanding of knitting principles. The mitten project demonstrates your ability to apply what you have learned.

## What does Level 2 require?

Level 2 adds colorwork, sock knitting, and garment construction.

- **Cost:** $190
- **Time limit:** 18 months
- **Requirements:** 3 book reviews, 2 reports, 19 swatches, 19 questions, 16 research questions on the history of knitting, 3 projects (Fair Isle wristlet, traditional argyle sock, vest)

The projects at this level introduce stranded colorwork and traditional knitting techniques. The book reviews and reports test your knowledge of knitting literature and theory.

## What does Level 3 require?

Level 3 is the final level. You design and knit original projects that demonstrate your mastery.

- **Cost:** $250
- **Time limit:** 24 months
- **Requirements:** 2 reports, 3 book reviews, 17 swatches, 16 questions, 2 self-designed projects (one hat in traditional Fair Isle style, one sweater in traditional Aran style)

At this level, you must design your own projects, not just follow existing patterns. The Aran sweater and Fair Isle hat must meet specific traditional technique standards. After passing this level, you receive the title of Master Knitter.

## What does it cost?

Total for all three levels is $590 for US members. Members outside the United States pay an additional $100 per level for shipping.

Level 1 - $150
Level 2 - $190
Level 3 - $250

The cost includes postage for your initial submission and one resubmission return. If you need more than one resubmission, you pay additional postage.

## How does the evaluation work?

After you submit a level, a review team evaluates your work. Two reviewers review Level 1, three review Level 2, and four review Level 3. You receive a letter with comments and all your materials returned. Items that do not pass can be reworked and resubmitted.

- Level 1 allows up to 2 resubmissions within 12 months
- Level 2 allows up to 3 resubmissions within 18 months
- Level 3 allows up to 3 resubmissions within 24 months

Expect 6 weeks to 3 months for review, depending on the level.

## How do I start?

1. Join [TKGA](https://tkga.org/membership/). You must be a member to participate.
2. Review the [Master Hand Knitting page](https://tkga.org/certification/master-hand-knitting/) and the [FAQ](https://tkga.org/certification/frequently-asked-questions-for-master-hand-knitting/).
3. Purchase Level 1 materials from the TKGA site.
4. Read the instructions carefully. Work through each requirement.
5. Contact [MKRouting@tkga.org](mailto:MKRouting@tkga.org) when you are ready to submit.
6. Wait for committee review and feedback. Rework any items that need improvement.
7. After passing Level 1, purchase Level 2 materials and repeat.

TKGA also offers a [Professional Knitter Certification](https://tkga.org/professional-knitter-certification/) and certifications for knitwear designers, judges, technical editors, and instructors if your goals include professional work rather than personal mastery.
