title: Structure of knitting
description: What is a knitted fabric, how it differs from woven fabric, the anatomy of stitches and rows, knit vs purl, garter vs stockinette, and why stitch shape matters for gauge
---

Knitting creates fabric from a single continuous yarn by forming interconnected loops. Each loop hangs on the loop below it, and the fabric is held on needles so the loops do not unravel. This structure gives knitted fabric stretch and recovery that woven fabric does not have.

## How is knitted fabric different from woven fabric?

Woven fabric is made from two independent sets of yarn or thread running perpendicular to each other (warp and weft). This produces a firmer, less elastic cloth. Knitted fabric is made from a single yarn forming a series of interconnected loops. This structure gives knitted fabric its elasticity.

- **Woven fabric:** Two perpendicular yarn sets, firm cloth, minimal stretch.
- **Knitted fabric:** Single continuous yarn, interconnected loops, elastic and stretchy.

## What is a stitch?

A stitch is one loop of yarn pulled through another loop. Each stitch sits on the needle as a live loop. When new stitches are worked, the old ones are released from the needle and held in place by the new loops passing through them.

## What is a row?

A row is a horizontal sequence of stitches worked across the needle. In flat knitting, you turn the work at the end of each row and work back in the opposite direction. In circular knitting, you work continuously in rounds without turning.

## What is the first row called?

The first row of stitches created on the needle is the **cast-on row**. It creates the foundation row of loops that all subsequent stitches build from. See [cast on methods](/knitting-reference/cast-on-methods/) for different ways to start.

## What is the last row called?

The final row that secures the stitches so they cannot unravel is the **bind-off** or **cast-off**. It locks each stitch closed while removing it from the needle.

## What is the shape of a knit stitch?

A knit stitch is **elongated**. It is taller than it is wide. This is important for gauge: stitch gauge (horizontal) and row gauge (vertical) are usually different numbers. One inch of knitting might have 5 stitches across and 7 rows down.

## What is a knit stitch?

A knit stitch is the most basic stitch in knitting. You insert the needle from front to back, wrap the yarn, and pull a new loop through. When you work every row as a knit stitch, the fabric is called **garter stitch**. It looks the same on both sides with raised horizontal ridges.

## What is a purl stitch?

A purl stitch is the reverse of a knit stitch. You bring the yarn to the front, insert the needle from back to front, and pull a new loop through. The front of a purl stitch looks like the back of a knit stitch, and vice versa.

## What is stockinette stitch?

Stockinette stitch is made by alternating a row of knit stitches with a row of purl stitches (in flat knitting). The side where the knit stitches face you is the **right side**. It shows smooth V-shaped columns. The side where the purl stitches face you is the **wrong side**. It shows bumpy horizontal bars.

- **Right side (knit side):** Smooth V shapes.
- **Wrong side (purl side):** Bumpy horizontal bars.

In circular knitting, stockinette is made by knitting every round (no purling needed) because you never turn the work.
