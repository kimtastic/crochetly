title: Troubleshooting knitting
description: Solutions for common knitting problems, such as dropped stitches, tension issues, curling edges, holes, twisted stitches, and joining new yarn, plus colorwork troubleshooting tips for beginners
---

Knitting is a relaxing craft, but sometimes things go wrong. This guide covers common problems and how to fix them.

## How do you fix general knitting problems?

Most common issues have simple fixes. Adjust your needle size, slow down, count stitches, and block your finished pieces.

### Dropped stitches
Use a crochet hook to pick up the dropped stitch and work it back up to the needle. For stockinette, catch the ladder from the front. For garter, alternate front and back.

### Tension too tight or loose
Adjust your needle size or consciously relax your hands. Looser tension often comes from rushing or gripping the needles too tightly.

### Splitting yarn
Use a smoother yarn or needles with rounded tips. Slow down and watch your stitches carefully.

### Skipping stitches
Count your stitches regularly. Use stitch markers every 10–20 stitches, especially in wide projects.

### Uneven edges
Use a selvedge edge stitch (slip the first stitch of every row) for a cleaner edge. Count stitches at the end of each row to make sure you did not drop or add any.

### Holes in your knitting
Holes often come from accidental yarn overs. Count stitches each row to catch extras. If holes are intentional (lace), check your pattern repeats carefully.

### Curling edges
Stockinette stitch naturally curls. Add a garter, seed stitch, or ribbed border to prevent curling. Blocking helps but does not fix curling permanently.

### Twisted stitches
Twisted stitches happen when you knit through the back loop by accident or when mounting stitches backward. Check your stitch mount regularly.

### Joining a new yarn ball
Join at the beginning of a row when possible. Leave 4–6 inch tails to weave in later. Avoid joining in the middle of a row for a cleaner look.

## How do you fix colorwork problems?

Colorwork adds complexity. Most issues come from tension, tangled yarns, or reading charts incorrectly.

### Puckered fabric (floats too tight)
Stretch the stitches on your right needle as you work the float. Catch floats every 2–3 stitches if they are long.

### Loose floats snagging
Keep floats snug but not tight. If you can catch your finger in a float, it is too loose.

### Tangled yarn balls
Wind your colors into separate balls or use yarn bobbins. Keep the working yarns organized to avoid knots.

### Misaligned pattern
Use a row counter and mark off chart rows as you finish them. Place stitch markers at pattern repeat boundaries.

## What are the bonus tips for troubleshooting?

Use stitch markers, count stitches often, watch tutorials, take breaks when your hands get tired, and be patient with yourself.

- **Use stitch markers** to keep track of your place.
- **Count stitches often** so you can catch mistakes early.
- **Use lifelines** (a scrap yarn threaded through a row of stitches) so you can rip back safely without losing stitches.
- **Watch tutorials or ask for help** in knitting communities.
- **Take breaks** when your hands get tired. Fatigue leads to mistakes.
- **Be patient with yourself.** Mistakes are part of learning.
