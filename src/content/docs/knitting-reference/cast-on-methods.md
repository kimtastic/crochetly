The cast-on is the foundation row of knitting. Different cast-on methods produce different edges: some are stretchy (ideal for socks and hat brims), some are decorative (for visible edges), and some are provisional (for later unzipping). This guide covers the most common methods grouped by what they do best.

## What is a slip knot?

Most cast-on methods start with a slip knot. An adjustable loop placed on one needle. Make a loop, pull a strand of yarn through it, and tighten it onto the needle. The slip knot counts as your first stitch.

## Basic cast-ons

These are the most common all-purpose cast-on methods. They work for most projects and are the first ones beginners learn.

### Long-tail cast on (double / German twisted / thumb method)

A versatile, sturdy cast-on that creates an even, elastic edge. You estimate a long tail, wrap the yarn around your thumb and index finger, then use the needle to pull loops through. The **German twisted** variant wraps the yarn twice around the thumb for extra elasticity. Ideal for socks and hat brims. The **thumb method** is the same technique with a slightly different hand position.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+longtail+cast+on)

### Backwards loop cast on (single / e-loop)

The simplest cast-on. Make a loop with the yarn and place it on the needle twists it. Quick and easy, but the edge is not very stable. It can look uneven and may stretch out of shape. Best for adding stitches in the middle of a row (like underarm stitches in a sweater), not for starting a project.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+backwards+loop+cast+on)

### Knit-on cast on

Knit stitches directly onto the right needle. Insert the right needle into the loop on the left needle as if to knit, wrap the yarn, pull through, and place the new loop onto the left needle. Simple and uses only one strand (no tail to estimate).

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+knit+on+cast+on)

### Cable cast on (alternate cable)

Creates a firm, decorative edge that looks like a braided cable. Insert the right needle between the last two stitches on the left needle (instead of through the last stitch), wrap, and pull through. The **alternate cable** cast-on purls the new stitches instead of knitting them, creating an even more defined edge.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+cable+cast+on)

### Left-handed cast on

A mirror of the long-tail method for left-handed knitters. The hand positions are swapped. The tail wraps around the right hand instead of the left, and the left needle does the work.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=left+handed+knitting+cast+on)

## Stretchy cast-ons for ribbing

These methods create edges that stretch as much as the ribbing pattern does. They are the best choice for socks, hat brims, cuffs, and neckbands.

### Tubular cast on (tubular chain / two-color Italian)

An invisible, stretchy edge that looks identical to a tubular bind-off. It creates a smooth, rolled edge with no visible cast-on ridge. The **two-color Italian** variant uses two colors for a braided, decorative edge. The most elastic cast-on available.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+tubular+cast+on)

### Chain cast on for K1 P1 rib

Uses a crochet chain worked behind the needle. Stitches are picked up from the bumps of the chain. Creates a stretchy, neat edge that flows directly into 1x1 ribbing.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+chain+cast+on+K1P1)

### Cast on for K2 P2 rib

The same concept as the K1 P1 chain cast-on, adapted for 2x2 ribbing. The crochet chain alternates in a 2-stitch pattern so the edge matches the rib structure.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+K2P2+cast+on)

## Invisible and provisional cast-ons

These methods create an edge that can be unzipped later, exposing live stitches for grafting or picking up in the opposite direction.

### Provisional cast on (open / crochet chain)

Uses scrap yarn and a crochet hook to create a chain. Knit stitches are picked up from the chain. When the chain is unzipped, the live stitches are revealed. Perfect for grafting (like the Kitchener stitch) or knitting in the opposite direction.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+provisional+cast+on)

### Invisible cast on

Creates a seamless starting edge without a crochet hook. The yarn is wrapped around the needle in a figure-eight pattern over a waste yarn strand. When the waste yarn is pulled out, live stitches are exposed.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+invisible+cast+on)

## Decorative cast-ons

These methods produce a visible, finished edge that becomes part of the design.

### Picot cast on

Creates a scalloped edge with small eyelets along the cast-on edge. Made by casting on a few stitches, binding off one, then slipping the remaining stitch back to the left needle and repeating.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=knitting+picot+cast+on)

### Guernsey cast on (channel island cast on)

A traditional cast-on from the Channel Islands (Guernsey sweaters). Alternates between two methods to create a decorative ridge above a rolled hem. The ridge acts as a visual border and adds durability to the edge.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=guernsey+cast+on+knitting)

### Two-color braided cast on

Uses two colors of yarn to create a braided, striped edge. The two strands are wrapped around the thumb in alternating colors, producing a twisted braid along the bottom.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=two+color+braided+cast+on+knitting)

### Three-color braided cast on

Same concept as the two-color braided cast-on but with three colors. The colors wrap around the thumb in sequence, creating a wider braided edge.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=three+color+braided+cast+on+knitting)

## Specialty cast-ons

These methods solve specific problems: knitting in the round from the center, starting an i-cord edge, or beginning toe-up socks.

### I-cord cast on

Cast on stitches and immediately work a small i-cord at the edge. Creates a rounded, finished tube edge. Great for button bands, bag handles, and edges that need structure.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=i+cord+cast+on+knitting)

### I-cord provisional cast on (for circular knitting)

An i-cord edge that can be unzipped to expose live stitches, specifically designed for circular knitting. The i-cord provides structure while the live stitches underneath are ready for grafting.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=i+cord+provisional+cast+on+circular+knitting)

### Turkish cast on (eastern / Emily Ocker)

A center-out cast-on for starting projects in the round (like toe-up socks or the top of a hat). Yarn is wrapped around two needles held together, then stitches are knit off in both directions.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=turkish+cast+on+knitting)
